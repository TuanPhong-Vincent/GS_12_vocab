LESSON 1 – PAST SIMPLE VS. PAST CONTINUOUS

Past simple vs. Past continuous: forms and core uses
  • We use the past simple to describe a completed action at a specific time in the past, or main chronological events in a life story. e.g. Steve Jobs dropped out of Reed College in 1972.
  • We use the past continuous to describe an action in progress at a specific time in the past, or to set the historical background/setting of a narrative. e.g. At 9 p.m. yesterday, Nam was reading the wartime diary of Dang Thuy Tram.
Tense / Structure Grammatical Form Core Usage & Context Example (Unit Context)
Past Simple S + V-ed / V2
S + didn't + V-inf		 Completed action; historical milestone; sequence of events Dr. Dang Thuy Tram volunteered to join the army at the age of 24.
Past Continuous S + was / were + V-ing
S + wasn't / weren't + V-ing		 Action in progress at a specific past point; background scene She was treating wounded soldiers in a remote field clinic.
Interrupted Action Past Cont. + WHEN + Past Simple
or WHILE + Past Cont., Past Simple		 A shorter action interrupts or occurs in the middle of a longer action The surgeon was operating on a patient when the power suddenly went out.
Parallel Actions Past Cont. + WHILE + Past Cont. Two continuous actions taking place simultaneously in the past While grandfather was fighting at the front, grandmother was managing the family farm.
⚠ WATCH OUT!
  • We do not use the past continuous with stative verbs (verbs describing states, thoughts, senses, or emotions: know, believe, understand, realize, remember, love, want, belong).
    • Correct: 'She knew her duty was dangerous.' (NOT: She was knowing...)
  • When describing a succession of quick consecutive actions in a biography, use the Past Simple for every verb in the sequence.
    • Example: 'He left Apple in 1985, founded NeXT, and purchased Pixar.'
Time clauses with When, While, and As
  • We frequently use time conjunctions (when, while, as) to link two clauses describing past actions.
  • As a rule of thumb, while and as introduce a longer background action (past continuous), whereas when often introduces a short or interrupting event (past simple).
Conjunction Clause Pattern Pedagogical Function Authentic Example
While / As While + S + was/were + V-ing, S + V-ed Introduces background duration before the main event While Steve Jobs was auditing calligraphy classes, he developed a love for typography.
When S + was/were + V-ing + when + S + V-ed Points to the specific moment an unexpected event happened The team was finalizing the animation software when the computer system crashed.
While (Simultaneous) While + Past Cont., Past Cont. Highlights two continuous activities in parallel While Nam was reading the diary, his brother was writing the historical summary.
When (Immediate) When + Past Simple, Past Simple Action in the 'when' clause triggers the next immediate action When the soldier found the diary, he decided not to burn it.
⚠ WATCH OUT!
  • If the time clause introduced by While or When stands at the beginning of the sentence, place a comma (,) after it. If it comes at the end, usually no comma is required.
    • While Dr. Tram was tending to the wounded, an explosion rocked the hill.
    • An explosion rocked the hill while Dr. Tram was tending to the wounded.
Spelling rules & verb inflection: -ed and -ing
  • Pay close attention to consonant doubling and silent 'e' deletion when forming past tense and present participles:
Base Form Rule Past Simple (-ed) Past Continuous (-ing) Examples
Consonant + single vowel + consonant (stressed) Double consonant + -ed Double consonant + -ing drop → dropped, dropping
occur → occurred, occurring
Ending in silent -e Add -d Drop -e + -ing devote → devoted, devoting
operate → operated, operating
Irregular Past Verbs V2 form (special change) Regular -ing addition write → wrote, writing
fly → flew, flying
